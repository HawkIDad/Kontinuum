# User Story
As the product owner of NoteBytez, I want to generate a data use policy so that end users understand how their data will be used by the application and by KwckSync (the company providing NotezBytez). This document is the canonical Privacy Policy incorporated by reference into the NoteBytez EULA (see [NoteBytez-EULA.md](../NoteBytez-EULA.md) Section 10). NoteBytez is a fully serverless app - KwickSync operates no backend of its own; all storage, sync, sharing, push notifications, and subscription entitlement checks happen through Apple's own infrastructure (CloudKit, the App Store, and APNs) - and is distributed worldwide, including to the EU/UK, which brings GDPR/UK GDPR obligations in addition to US state privacy laws.

# Success Factors
1. KwickSync will not access note content or other data created by the End User within the App. This "zero access" claim is limited to End User-generated Content; it does not extend to the operational data described in Success Factor 4.
2. Data (notes and ancilliary data) will be stored on the users device(s) or in the Apple iCloud - using CloudKit as the storage container and data sync mechanism.
3. Because KwickSync does not have access to the users Content, KwickSync will not share End User Content with any 3rd party. This is separate from the App's own sharing feature, where an End User directly and intentionally shares a note with another NoteBytez user of their choosing - that is not third-party sharing by KwickSync.
4. KwickSync does have visibility into the following limited, non-Content data, each of which the policy will disclose: (a) subscription/purchase entitlement status reported by Apple so the App can grant or deny access; (b) customer support communications an End User voluntarily sends to KwickSync; and (c) aggregated, typically anonymized crash/diagnostic analytics shared via Apple's opt-in developer analytics.
5. The policy will confirm that NoteBytez is fully serverless - KwickSync operates no backend infrastructure of its own - which is what makes the zero-access claim in Success Factor 1 possible.
6. The policy will state that KwickSync does not sell End User personal information and does not use End User data for advertising or marketing purposes, and that KwickSync does not send marketing/promotional communications.
7. The policy will state that the App is not directed at children under the age of 13, mirroring the EULA's minimum age requirement, and will note that EU/UK End Users under their country's applicable age of digital consent (13-16, depending on country) require parental/guardian consent.
8. The policy will disclose data retention: End User Content remains in the End User's own iCloud account and on-device indefinitely, including after subscription termination — the App always provides read-only access and export of existing Content, with no forced deletion timeline (matching the App's existing "never delete on lapse" design, Security design doc NoteBytez20260907v1-Security.md resolutions G16/G17, and EULA Section 15). Support communications are retained only as long as necessary to resolve the inquiry and for a reasonable period thereafter.
9. The policy will identify KwickSync as the data controller and Apple as a data processor/infrastructure provider (for CloudKit, App Store, and APNs) for GDPR purposes, and will state the lawful basis for processing (contract performance for core functionality; legitimate interest for support and diagnostics).
10. The policy will disclose that, because NoteBytez is distributed worldwide, Apple's infrastructure may store and process data in multiple countries, subject to Apple's own safeguards for international data transfers (e.g. Standard Contractual Clauses).
11. The policy will state that KwickSync relies on the GDPR Article 27(2) exemption from appointing an EU/UK representative, on the basis that its processing is occasional, low-risk, does not involve special category data at scale, and is not large-scale.
12. The policy will describe GDPR/UK GDPR data subject rights (access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, objection) and CCPA/California rights (right to know, delete, and to non-discrimination; confirming KwickSync does not sell or share personal information), and how an End User can exercise them given KwickSync's limited data visibility.
13. The policy will describe security measures: Apple CloudKit encrypts End User Content in transit and at rest, and KwickSync itself never receives a copy of that Content.
14. The policy will describe how KwickSync will notify End Users of material changes to this policy, consistent with the EULA's Section 21 (Changes to This Agreement).
15. The policy will provide a contact method for privacy-related inquiries and data subject rights requests.
16. A code-level compliance audit (2026-09-19) verified the app matches these claims: no KwickSync-operated backend, no third-party analytics/tracking SDKs, CloudKit private/shared database only (no public database, no Discover User Identity usage), on-device-only StoreKit entitlement checks, CloudKit-subscription-driven push with no device token leaving the device, OSLog-only local logging, and no marketing-email collection. Two gaps found and fixed as part of this audit:
    - `PaywallView.swift`'s Terms of Use / Privacy Policy links pointed at placeholder `notebytez.app` URLs that don't exist; they now open the real bundled EULA/Data Use Policy documents in-app via a new `LegalDocumentView`.
    - The whole `NoteBytez/Docs` tree (including internal security design rationale and bug reports) and `NoteBytez/ARCHITECTURE.md` were being bundled into the shipped app by Xcode's synchronized-group default membership; the Xcode project now explicitly excludes everything under those paths except the two documents meant to ship (`NoteBytez-EULA.md`, `NoteBytez-Data-Use-Policy.md`), verified by inspecting an actual built `.app`'s contents before and after. This exclusion list is enumerated per-file (Xcode's synchronized groups don't support folder-level exceptions) — a new file added to `Docs/Plans`, `Docs/Bugs`, `Docs/Localization`, or `Docs/Templates` will ship in the app bundle by default unless someone remembers to add it to `project.pbxproj`'s membership exceptions.
